Suppose an alpha particle accelerated by a potential of V volt is allowed to collide with a nucleus of atomic number Z, then the distance of closest approach of alpha particle to the nucleus is

```markdown
At the distance of closest approach, the entire kinetic energy of the alpha particle is converted into electrostatic potential energy.

For an alpha particle,

$$
\text{K.E.} = 2eV
$$

Electrostatic potential energy at the closest distance $r$ is

$$
U = \frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{(2e)(Ze)}{r}
$$

Equating kinetic and potential energies,

$$
2eV
=
\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{2Ze^2}{r}
$$

$$
r
=
\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\frac{Ze}{V}
$$

Using

$$
\frac{e}{4\pi\epsilon_0}
=
14.4\ \text{eV}\,\text{\AA}
$$

we get

$$
r = \frac{14.4\,Z}{V}\ \text{\AA}
$$

Hence, **the distance of closest approach is**

$$
\boxed{\frac{14.4\,Z}{V}\ \text{\AA}}
$$
```
