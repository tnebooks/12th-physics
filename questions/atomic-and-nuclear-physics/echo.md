  - 'M(N,Z)=NMn+ZMp-Bc^2'
answers:
  - 'M(N,Z)=NMn+ZMp+B/c^2'
---

Mp denotes the mass of the proton and Mn denotes mass of a neutron. A given nucleus of binding energy B, contains Z protons and N neutrons. The mass M(N,Z) of the nucleus is given by(where c is the speed of light)

```markdown
Binding energy is the energy released when nucleons combine to form a nucleus.

According to Einstein's mass-energy relation,

$$
B=\Delta m\,c^2
$$

Hence,

$$
\Delta m=\frac{B}{c^2}
$$

The mass of the nucleus is less than the sum of the masses of its constituent nucleons by the mass defect:

$$
M(N,Z)=Nm_n+Zm_p-\frac{B}{c^2}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{M(N,Z)=Nm_n+Zm_p-\frac{B}{c^2}}
$$

**Note:** This is the physically correct relation. If your answer key shows \(+\frac{B}{c^2}\), it is likely a misprint.
```
