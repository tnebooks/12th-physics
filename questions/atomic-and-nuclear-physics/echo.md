**$M_p$ denotes the mass of the proton and $M_n$ denotes mass of a neutron. A given nucleus of binding energy $B$, contains $Z$ protons and $N$ neutrons. The mass $M(N,Z)$ of the nucleus is given by (where $c$ is the speed of light)**

```markdown
The binding energy ($B$) of a nucleus is the energy equivalent of its mass defect ($\Delta m$). 

The mass defect is the difference between the total mass of the individual, separated nucleons (protons and neutrons) and the actual rest mass of the combined nucleus ($M(N,Z)$):
$$\Delta m = (Z M_p + N M_n) - M(N,Z)$$

According to Einstein's mass-energy equivalence principle ($E = mc^2$), the binding energy is:
$$B = \Delta m \cdot c^2$$
$$B = \left[ (Z M_p + N M_n) - M(N,Z) \right] c^2$$

To find the expression for the actual mass of the nucleus $M(N,Z)$ with proper subscript formatting, we rearrange this equation:
$$\frac{B}{c^2} = Z M_p + N M_n - M(N,Z)$$

$$M(N,Z) = N M_n + Z M_p - \frac{B}{c^2}$$

This demonstrates that the mass of a stable nucleus is always less than the sum of the masses of its constituent nucleons by an amount equal to $\frac{B}{c^2}$.

Correct answer is **M(N,Z) = N M_n + Z M_p - B/c^2**.
```
