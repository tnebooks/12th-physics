---
choices:
  - "A and B have the same decay rate initially"
  - "A and B decay at the same rate always"
  - "A will decay at faster rate than B."
answers:
  - "B will decay at faster rate than A"
---

The half-life period of a radioactive
element A is same as the mean life
time of another radioactive element B.
Initially both have the same number of
atoms. Then


```markdown
Given,

$$
T_{1/2,A}=\tau_B
$$

where $\tau_B$ is the mean life of radioactive element B.

The relation between mean life and half-life is

$$
\tau=\frac{T_{1/2}}{\ln 2}
\approx 1.44\,T_{1/2}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\tau_B=T_{1/2,A}
\Rightarrow
T_{1/2,B}=0.693\,T_{1/2,A}
$$

Thus, the half-life of B is less than that of A.

A smaller half-life means a larger decay constant and hence a faster rate of decay.

Therefore, **B will decay at a faster rate than A.**
```
