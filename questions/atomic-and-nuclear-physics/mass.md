A radioactive nucleus (initial mass number A and atomic number Z) emits two α-particles and 2 positrons. The ratio of number of neutrons to that of proton in the final nucleus will be

```markdown
For each $\alpha$-particle emitted,

$$
A \rightarrow A-4,\qquad Z \rightarrow Z-2
$$

After emission of $2$ $\alpha$-particles,

$$
A \rightarrow A-8,\qquad Z \rightarrow Z-4
$$

For each positron emission,

$$
Z \rightarrow Z-1
$$

After emission of $2$ positrons,

$$
Z \rightarrow Z-6
$$

Therefore, in the final nucleus,

Proton number

$$
= Z-6
$$

Neutron number

$$
= (A-8)-(Z-6)
= A-Z-2
$$

Hence, the ratio of neutrons to protons is

$$
\boxed{\frac{A-Z-2}{Z-6}}
$$
```
