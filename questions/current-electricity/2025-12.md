A thin conducting spherical shell of radius $R$ has a charge $Q$ which is uniformly distributed on its surface. The correct plot for electrostatic potential $V$ versus distance $r$ due to this spherical shell is:

```markdown
Inside a conducting spherical shell ($r \le R$), the electric field is zero, which means the electrostatic potential remains constant and equal to its value on the surface:
$$V = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{Q}{R} \quad (r \le R)$$

Outside the shell ($r > R$):
$$V = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{Q}{r} \propto \frac{1}{r}$$

Thus, the curve is flat (horizontal line) from $0$ to $R$, followed by a hyperbolic decay as $\frac{1}{r}$ for $r > R$.
```
