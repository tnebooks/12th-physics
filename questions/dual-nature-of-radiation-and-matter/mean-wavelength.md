**If the mean wavelength of light from sun is taken as $550 \text{ nm}$ and its mean power as $3.8 \times 10^{26} \text{ W}$, then the number of photons emitted per second from the sun is of the order**

```markdown
**Given values:**
* Mean wavelength, $\lambda = 550 \text{ nm} = 550 \times 10^{-9} \text{ m}$
* Power of the sun, $P = 3.8 \times 10^{26} \text{ W}$
* Planck's constant, $h = 6.6 \times 10^{-34} \text{ Js}$
* Speed of light, $c = 3 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}$

**Step 1: Calculate the energy of a single photon ($E$)**
$$E = \frac{hc}{\lambda}$$
$$E = \frac{(6.6 \times 10^{-34}) \times (3 \times 10^8)}{550 \times 10^{-9}}$$
$$E = \frac{19.8 \times 10^{-26}}{5.5 \times 10^{-7}} = 3.6 \times 10^{-19} \text{ J}$$

**Step 2: Calculate the number of photons emitted per second ($n$)**
The total power $P$ represents the total energy emitted per second. This is equal to the number of photons emitted per second ($n$) multiplied by the energy of one photon ($E$):
$$P = n \cdot E \implies n = \frac{P}{E}$$
$$n = \frac{3.8 \times 10^{26}}{3.6 \times 10^{-19}}$$
$$n \approx 1.055 \times 10^{45}$$

Therefore, the number of photons emitted per second is of the order **$10^{45}$**.
```
