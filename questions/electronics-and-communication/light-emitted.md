**The light emitted in an LED is due to**

```markdown
The light emitted in an LED (Light Emitting Diode) is due to the **recombination of charge carriers** (electrons and holes) at the p-n junction.

When an LED is forward biased:
- Electrons from the n-region move into the p-region.
- Holes from the p-region move into the n-region.
- When these electrons and holes recombine at the junction, energy is released in the form of photons (light).

Other options:
- **"Large current capacity"** → Not a characteristic of LEDs (LEDs operate at low current).
- **"Amplification of light falling at the junction"** → This is the principle of a phototransistor or photodiode (light amplification), not an LED.
- **"Reflection of light due to lens action"** → LEDs may have lenses to focus light, but this is not the cause of light emission.

Therefore, the correct answer is **"Recombination of charge carriers"**.
```
