Two identical point charges of magnitude $-q$ are fixed as shown in the figure below. A third charge $+q$ is placed midway between the two charges at the point P. Suppose this charge $+q$ is displaced a small distance from the point P in the directions indicated by the arrows, in which direction(s) will $+q$ be stable with respect to the displacement?

![Image](./image.png)

```markdown
At point P, the net force on the central charge $+q$ is zero because the two $-q$ charges exert equal and opposite forces on it.

For stability, we check if a small displacement leads to a restoring force (toward P).

- **Displacement along A₁ or A₂ (horizontal)**:  
  If $+q$ moves toward one of the $-q$ charges, the attraction from that nearby charge increases, and the repulsion/attraction from the farther charge decreases. The net force is **away from P**, so the displacement is **unstable** in these directions.

- **Displacement along B₁ or B₂ (vertical)**:  
  If $+q$ moves perpendicular to the line joining the two $-q$ charges, the forces from the two charges have components that point **toward P** (restoring force). Hence, the charge is **stable** for small vertical displacements.

Therefore, the charge $+q$ is stable only for displacements along **B₁ and B₂**.

So, the correct is ** B₁ and B₂**.
```
