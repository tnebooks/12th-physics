**Two identical point charges of magnitude –q are fixed as shown in the figure below. A third charge +q is placed midway between the two charges at the point P. Suppose this charge +q is displaced a small distance from the point P in the directions indicated by the arrows, in which direction(s) will +q be stable with respect to the displacement?**

![](qns-1.png)

```markdown
At point P (midway between the two fixed –q charges), the net force on +q is zero because the attractive forces from the two –q charges are equal and opposite.

Now consider small displacements:

- **Along A1 and A2 (horizontal direction, i.e., along the line joining the two –q charges):**  
  If +q is displaced towards one –q, the attraction from that nearer –q becomes stronger than the attraction from the farther –q. The net force pulls +q further in the same direction → **unstable equilibrium**.

- **Along B1 and B2 (perpendicular to the line joining the two –q charges, i.e., vertical direction):**  
  If +q is displaced perpendicularly, the two –q charges pull it back towards the center because the components of forces along the displacement direction oppose the displacement. The net restoring force acts towards P → **stable equilibrium**.

Thus, +q is stable only for displacements in the **B1 and B2** directions.

Correct answer: **B1 and B2**
```
