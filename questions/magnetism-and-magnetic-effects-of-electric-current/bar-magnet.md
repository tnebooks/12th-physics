**A bar magnet of length l and magnetic moment $p_m$ is bent in the form of an arc as shown in figure. The new magnetic dipole moment will be**

![](qns-9.png)

```markdown
The magnetic dipole moment (M) = pole strength (q_m) × effective distance between poles.

Initially, for a straight bar magnet of length l:
M = p_m = q_m × l  →  q_m = p_m / l

When bent into an arc, the length of the arc equals the original length l.
If the arc is a semicircle of radius R, then:
Arc length = πR = l  →  R = l/π

The effective distance between the poles is the straight-line distance between the ends of the arc, which is the diameter:
Effective distance = 2R = 2l/π

New magnetic moment:
M_new = q_m × (2l/π) = (p_m / l) × (2l/π) = 2p_m / π

Therefore, the new magnetic dipole moment = 2/π pm.

(Note: The answer "3/π pm" given in the options appears to be incorrect based on standard physics. The correct derivation gives 2/π pm. If the arc is a different angle, the formula would be M_new = p_m × (2 sin(θ/2))/θ, where θ is the angle subtended. For a semicircle, θ = π, giving 2/π pm.)
```
