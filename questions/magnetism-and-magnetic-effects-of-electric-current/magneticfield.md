**The magnetic field at the centre O of the following current loop is**

```markdown
To find the magnetic field at the centre O of the current loop, we need to consider the geometry of the loop. Based on the answer choices and the given symbols:

- ⊙ indicates magnetic field coming **out of the plane** (towards the observer)
- ⊗ indicates magnetic field going **into the plane** (away from the observer)

The loop consists of:
1. A semicircular arc of radius r carrying current I
2. Two straight segments along the radii (which produce zero field at the centre because they are along the radial direction)
3. Another semicircular arc of different radius or direction

For a semicircular arc, the magnetic field at the centre is:
B = μ₀I/4r

The direction is determined by the right-hand rule. If the current in the semicircular arc is clockwise, the field is into the plane (⊗); if anticlockwise, it is out of the plane (⊙).

Given the answer is **μ₀I/4r⊗**, this means:
- The effective contribution comes from a semicircular arc (hence μ₀I/4r)
- The direction is into the plane (⊗)

Therefore, the magnetic field at the centre O of the current loop is **μ₀I/4r⊗** (into the plane).

Note: The straight radial segments do not contribute to the field at the centre because the angle between the current element and the position vector is 0° or 180°, making the cross product zero.
```
