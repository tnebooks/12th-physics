**The potential energy of magnetic dipole whose dipole moment is $\vec{p}_m = (-0.5 \hat{i} + 0.4 \hat{j})\text{ Am}^2$ kept in uniform magnetic field $\vec{B} = 0.2 \hat{i}\text{ T}$**

```markdown
The potential energy ($U$) of a magnetic dipole placed in a uniform magnetic field is given by the scalar (dot) product formula:
$$U = -\vec{p}_m \cdot \vec{B}$$

Given values:
* Magnetic dipole moment, $\vec{p}_m = -0.5 \hat{i} + 0.4 \hat{j}$
* Magnetic field, $\vec{B} = 0.2 \hat{i}$

Now, let's substitute these vectors into the potential energy formula:
$$U = - \left[ (-0.5 \hat{i} + 0.4 \hat{j}) \cdot (0.2 \hat{i}) \right]$$

Since the dot product of orthogonal unit vectors is zero ($\hat{i} \cdot \hat{j} = 0$) and $\hat{i} \cdot \hat{i} = 1$, we only multiply the corresponding components along the x-axis ($\hat{i}$):
$$U = - \left[ (-0.5 \times 0.2) + (0.4 \times 0) \right]$$
$$U = - \left[ -0.1 \right]$$
$$U = 0.1\text{ J}$$

Therefore, the potential energy of the magnetic dipole is **0.1 J**.
```
