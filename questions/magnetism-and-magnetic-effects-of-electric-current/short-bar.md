**Two short bar magnets have magnetic moments 1.20 Am² and 1.00 Am² respectively. They are kept on horizontal table parallel to each other with their north poles pointing towards south. They have a common magnetic equator and are separated by a distance of 20.0 cm. The value of the resultant horizontal magnetic induction at the mid-point O of the line joining their centres is (Horizontal components of Earth’s magnetic induction is 3.6 × 10⁻⁵ Wb m⁻²)**

```markdown
**Step 1: Understand the geometry**

- Both magnets are parallel with their north poles pointing towards south.
- They have a common magnetic equator, meaning the point O lies on the equatorial line of **both** magnets.
- Distance between the centres = 20.0 cm, so O is at 10.0 cm = 0.10 m from each magnet.

**Step 2: Magnetic field on the equatorial line of a short bar magnet**

For a short bar magnet, the magnetic induction at a point on the equatorial line is:
B = (μ₀/4π) × m / r³  (direction opposite to the magnetic moment)

μ₀/4π = 10⁻⁷ T m/A

**Step 3: Calculate field due to each magnet**

Magnet 1: m₁ = 1.20 Am², r = 0.10 m
B₁ = 10⁻⁷ × 1.20 / (0.10)³ = 10⁻⁷ × 1.20 / 0.001 = 1.20 × 10⁻⁴ T

Magnet 2: m₂ = 1.00 Am², r = 0.10 m
B₂ = 10⁻⁷ × 1.00 / (0.10)³ = 10⁻⁷ × 1.00 / 0.001 = 1.00 × 10⁻⁴ T

**Step 4: Direction of fields**

Since north poles point towards south, the magnetic moments are along south. On the equatorial line, the field is opposite to the magnetic moment, i.e., towards north. Both fields act in the **same direction** (towards north).

So, resultant field from the two magnets:
B_magnets = B₁ + B₂ = (1.20 + 1.00) × 10⁻⁴ = 2.20 × 10⁻⁴ T

**Step 5: Include Earth's horizontal component**

Earth's horizontal component B_H = 3.6 × 10⁻⁵ T acts towards north (assuming standard direction).

Since both the magnet's field and Earth's field act in the same direction:
B_resultant = B_magnets + B_H = 2.20 × 10⁻⁴ + 0.36 × 10⁻⁴ = 2.56 × 10⁻⁴ T

Therefore, the resultant horizontal magnetic induction at O is **2.56 × 10⁻⁴ Wb m⁻²**.
```
