**Two identical coils, each with N turns and radius R are placed coaxially at a distance R as shown in the figure. If I is the current passing through the loops in the same direction, then the magnetic field at a point P at a distance of R/2 from the centre of each coil is**

![](qns-7.png)

```markdown
**Step 1: Magnetic field on the axis of a single coil**

For a coil of radius R with N turns carrying current I, the magnetic field at a distance x from its centre along the axis is:

B = (μ₀ N I R²) / [2(R² + x²)^(3/2)]

**Step 2: Position of point P**

The two coils are separated by a distance R. Point P is at a distance R/2 from the centre of **each** coil.

So, x = R/2 from each coil.

**Step 3: Field due to each coil at P**

B₁ = B₂ = (μ₀ N I R²) / [2(R² + (R/2)²)^(3/2)]

= (μ₀ N I R²) / [2(R² + R²/4)^(3/2)]

= (μ₀ N I R²) / [2(5R²/4)^(3/2)]

= (μ₀ N I R²) / [2 × (5^(3/2)/8) × R³]

= (μ₀ N I) / [2 × (5√5/8) × R]

= (μ₀ N I) / [(5√5/4) R]

= 4 μ₀ N I / (5√5 R)

**Step 4: Direction of fields**

Since the current is in the **same direction** in both coils, the magnetic fields at P due to both coils are in the **same direction** (according to the right-hand rule).

Therefore, the net magnetic field at P:

B_net = B₁ + B₂ = 2 × [4 μ₀ N I / (5√5 R)]

B_net = 8 μ₀ N I / (5√5 R)

B_net = 8 N μ₀ I / (5^(3/2) R)   [since √5 = 5^(1/2), so 5√5 = 5^(3/2)]

Therefore, the magnetic field at point P is **8Nμ₀I/5^(3/2)R**.
```
