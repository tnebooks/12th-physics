**An air bubble in glass slab of refractive index 1.5 (near normal incidence) is 5 cm deep when viewed from one surface and 3 cm deep when viewed from the opposite face. The thickness of the slab is:**

```markdown
When looking at an object inside a denser medium from a rarer medium (like looking into a glass slab from air), the object appears closer to the surface than it actually is due to refraction. This perceived depth is called the **apparent depth**.

The relationship between the real depth, apparent depth, and the refractive index ($\mu$) is given by the formula:
$$\mu = \frac{\text{Real Depth}}{\text{Apparent Depth}}$$
$$\text{Real Depth} = \mu \times \text{Apparent Depth}$$

Let's calculate the real distance of the bubble from both surfaces:
* **From the first surface:** $$\text{Real Depth}_1 = 1.5 \times 5\text{ cm} = 7.5\text{ cm}$$
* **From the opposite surface:** $$\text{Real Depth}_2 = 1.5 \times 3\text{ cm} = 4.5\text{ cm}$$

The total thickness of the glass slab ($t$) is the sum of these two real depths:
$$t = \text{Real Depth}_1 + \text{Real Depth}_2$$
$$t = 7.5\text{ cm} + 4.5\text{ cm} = 12\text{ cm}$$

Therefore, the actual thickness of the slab is **12 cm**.
```
