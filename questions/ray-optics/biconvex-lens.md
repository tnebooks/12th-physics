**When a biconvex lens of glass having refractive index 1.47 is dipped in a liquid, it acts as a plane sheet of glass. This implies that the liquid must have refractive index,**

```markdown
According to the **Lens Maker's Formula**, the focal length ($f$) of a lens immersed in a surrounding medium is given by:

$$\frac{1}{f} = \left(\frac{\mu_{\text{lens}}}{\mu_{\text{medium}}} - 1\right) \left(\frac{1}{R_1} - \frac{1}{R_2}\right)$$

Where:
* $\mu_{\text{lens}}$ is the refractive index of the lens material ($1.47$).
* $\mu_{\text{medium}}$ is the refractive index of the surrounding liquid.

When the biconvex lens behaves like a **plane sheet of glass**, it means light rays pass straight through it without bending (converging or diverging). For a plane sheet, the focal length becomes infinite ($\infty$).

Substituting $f = \infty$ into the formula:
$$\frac{1}{\infty} = 0$$

Therefore:
$$\left(\frac{\mu_{\text{lens}}}{\mu_{\text{medium}}} - 1\right) = 0$$

$$\frac{\mu_{\text{lens}}}{\mu_{\text{medium}}} = 1$$

$$\mu_{\text{lens}} = \mu_{\text{medium}}$$

Since the light does not experience any change in optical density at the boundary, no refraction occurs, making the lens virtually invisible in the liquid. Thus, the refractive index of the liquid must be **equal to that of glass** ($1.47$).

```
