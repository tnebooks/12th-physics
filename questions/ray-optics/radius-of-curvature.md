**The radius of curvature of curved surface at a thin planoconvex lens is 10 cm and the refractive index is 1.5. If the plane surface is silvered, then the focal length will be,**

```markdown
When the plane surface of a plano-convex lens is silvered, the system acts as a mirror. The light travels through the lens, reflects off the silvered back surface, and travels back through the lens. The effective power ($P$) of this combination is:
$$P = 2P_L + P_M$$

In terms of focal length, this becomes:
$$-\frac{1}{F} = \frac{2}{f_L} - \frac{1}{f_M}$$

Given values:
* Refractive index, $\mu = 1.5$
* Radius of curved surface, $R_1 = +10\text{ cm}$
* Radius of plane surface, $R_2 = \infty$

**1. Focal length of the lens ($f_L$):**
According to the Lens Maker's formula:
$$\frac{1}{f_L} = (\mu - 1) \left(\frac{1}{R_1} - \frac{1}{R_2}\right)$$
$$\frac{1}{f_L} = (1.5 - 1) \left(\frac{1}{10} - \frac{1}{\infty}\right) = 0.5 \times \frac{1}{10} = \frac{1}{20}$$
$$f_L = 20\text{ cm}$$

**2. Focal length of the mirror ($f_M$):**
Because the silvered surface is flat (plane), its radius of curvature is infinity ($\infty$). Consequently, its focal length is also infinity.
$$f_M = \infty \implies \frac{1}{f_M} = 0$$

**3. Effective focal length ($F$):**
Substitute these values back into the equivalent focal length equation:
$$-\frac{1}{F} = 2\left(\frac{1}{20}\right) - 0$$
$$-\frac{1}{F} = \frac{1}{10}$$
$$F = -10\text{ cm}$$

*(The negative sign indicates that the system behaves as a converging / concave mirror).*

Therefore, the focal length of the system is **10 cm**.
```
