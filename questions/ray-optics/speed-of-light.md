**The speed of light in an isotropic medium depends on,**

```markdown
The speed of light in a medium ($v$) is given by the product of its frequency ($\nu$) and wavelength ($\lambda$):
$$v = \nu \lambda$$

* **Isotropic Medium:** An isotropic medium has the same physical and optical properties (like the refractive index) in all directions.
* **Role of Wavelength:** When light travels from one medium to another, its frequency remains constant because frequency is a fundamental property of the light source. However, both its speed and wavelength change.

According to **Cauchy's Equation**, the refractive index ($\mu$) of a material depends on the wavelength ($\lambda$) of the incident light:
$$\mu = A + \frac{B}{\lambda^2} + \frac{C}{\lambda^4} + \dots$$

Since the speed of light in a medium is related to the refractive index by the formula $v = \frac{c}{\mu}$, and the refractive index varies with wavelength, the speed of light in the medium ultimately depends on **its wavelength**. It is independent of the light's intensity, nature of propagation, or the motion of the source.

Correct answer is **its wavelength**.
```
