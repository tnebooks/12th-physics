In a Young’s double-slit experiment, the slit separation is doubled. To maintain the same fringe spacing on the screen, the screen-to-slit distance D must be changed to,


```markdown
In Young’s double-slit experiment, the fringe width is given by

$$
\beta = \frac{D\lambda}{d}
$$

where

- $D$ = distance between the slits and the screen
- $d$ = separation between the slits
- $(\lambda$) = wavelength of light

To keep the fringe width unchanged, $(\beta$) must remain constant.

If the slit separation is doubled from $d$ to $2d$, then

$$
\frac{D\lambda}{d} = \frac{D'\lambda}{2d}
$$

Solving,

$$
D' = 2D
$$

Hence, **to maintain the same fringe spacing, the screen-to-slit distance must be increased to $2D$.**
```
