```markdown
When one of the slits in Young's double-slit experiment is covered with a glass plate, the central maximum shifts **upwards** (or towards the slit covered with the glass plate).

**Reason:**
- The glass plate introduces an extra optical path length for the light passing through that slit.
- This causes a phase difference between the two beams at the original central position.
- To compensate for this phase difference and restore constructive interference, the central maximum shifts towards the side where the glass plate is placed (i.e., upwards if the upper slit is covered).

If the upper slit is covered with the glass plate:
- The central maximum shifts **upwards**.
- If the lower slit is covered, it shifts **downwards**.

Since the figure (not shown here) presumably has the upper slit covered, the correct answer is **"get shifted upwards"**.
```
